# Water adventure visual revision and shared boarding

The player's review rejected the schematic scenery. This revision changes only
these ten new models: continuous eroded cliffs and floating-island contours,
irregular river boulders, compound tree crowns, curved serrated palm leaves,
hanging plants, a continuous foamy barrel-wave lip, a substantial open-drain
crystal-bowl cradle, faceted crystals and flower arcades around the carousel.
The broad water routes retain the shared native reflection/light probe, with
less repetitive foam and reduced high-frequency surface contrast.

These are original stylized 3D models. They do not reproduce every detail of
the supplied painted reference. The old sixty registered water models, all
nineteen islands, existing purchase addresses/prices and approved cats/wildlife
are preserved. Nine shared immutable 128² material maps cost about 768 KiB with
mipmaps, an increase of 256 KiB over the preceding six-map content release.
There are no extra reflection passes, per-frame textures, lowered DPR or
changes to the isolated performance experiment.

Seven shared attractions now wait fifteen real seconds after the first actual
seating. Free local residents walk to fill the remaining seats automatically.
The existing ground path continues along the authored stairs/deck; late arrivals
wait for the next boat. All passengers share one absolute departure and wet-route
clock. Work, sleep, care, housing, trips and manual activities retain priority.
Recruiting adds no AI and plans at most two candidate paths per half-second.
Single-person rides preserve their previous independent headway.

The focused suite covers mounting, timer boundaries, later arrivals, next-boat
waiting, cancellation, dry-route continuity for every shared seat, common native
raft motion, camera candidates and actual largest-cat paw/deck clearance.
The complete Node suite passed 897/897. Raw rendering, private phone/SQLite and
publication evidence accompanies the final release record. Software WebGL
checks establish correctness and resource behavior, not physical-phone FPS.
